Circuits and bridges, as a cellular automaton

Draw a line on the hexagon field. Touch your own line to close a circuit, and the inside floods. Run from the edge back to the edge to make a bridge, and the side with the shorter stretch of border floods. Every cell runs one local rule on its six neighbours; nothing in it can see the whole board. Source on GitHub

The rule, cell by cell

The only input is “the line's next cell is here”, one cell per step, the way a line grows in the game. Each line cell knows the cell before it and, once placed, the one after, so the line is a wire that signals can run along. The new head looks round the step after it lands.

Circuit: the head touches its own line

  1. Close. The head is next to a cell of its line other than the one before it or the one before that (that is only a sharp turn). That cell is the anchor; the loop runs from it to the head.
  2. Count the turns. A signal walks back from the head to the anchor, adding the turn the loop makes at each cell in 60° steps. A simple loop turns exactly ±360°, which is ±6 steps, so the sum mod 8 is 6 (counter-clockwise) or 2 (clockwise). Three bits do it, however long the loop.
  3. Fill the inside. The anchor adds its own turn and the head's, and keeps the answer. The head reads it from beside it and sends it back round the loop; each cell it passes floods its neighbours on the inside of its own turn.

Bridge: the line reaches the border again

  1. Arm. The head lands on the border, and the line had been on the border and left it. The head fires a pulse both ways round the border ring.
  2. Echo. A pulse moves one ring cell per step and turns back at the line. Old lines don't stop it.
  3. Judge. The first echo back came along the shorter arc, at 2 · (cells on it) + 2 steps. The head floods that side. Two at once is a tie, and nothing floods.

The flood spreads to every neighbour that is not outside and not the line. Once the board is quiet the line becomes an old line, which pulses and floods pass over. The readout checks every round against a fill computed over the whole board.
