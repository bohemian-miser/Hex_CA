Shorter-side flood, as a cellular automaton

A line of cells crosses the hexagon field edge to edge. Every cell runs the same local rule on its six neighbours, and between them they flood the side whose stretch of the border is shorter. Nothing in the rule can see the whole board. Source on GitHub

Field
fresh line old line pulse flood border ring

The rule, cell by cell

  1. Border. A cell with an outside neighbour knows it is on the border ring. On a hexagon-shaped field each border cell has exactly two border neighbours, so the ring is a cycle every cell can follow locally.
  2. Arm. A fresh-line cell on the border is a foot. It arms, and next step both of its ring neighbours pick up a pulse.
  3. Race. A pulse moves one ring cell per step, away from where it came from. It stores only that direction (6 bits per cell). Pulses pass through each other and through old lines.
  4. Judge. A listening foot waits for a pulse from its partner. The first to arrive came along the shorter arc, at step 2 + (cells on that arc). Both feet hear at the same step, so they always agree. Two at once is a tie, and nothing floods.
  5. Flood. The ring cell a decided foot points at floods; flood spreads to every neighbour that is not outside and not the fresh line. Old lines don't stop it.

“Add another line” is the one input from outside the automaton: the fresh line becomes an old line and a new one is drawn, as when a player finishes a line in the game. The readout checks every round against a global reference fill.
